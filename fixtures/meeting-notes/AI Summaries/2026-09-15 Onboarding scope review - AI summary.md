## Discussion summary

### Establishing a bounded first-week scope

The immediate need is a guide that helps new Support colleagues navigate their
first week without assuming they already understand the team's tools and
working practices. The checklist should support that guide rather than become
a separate training program. Participants distinguished this deliverable from
rewriting every training document or settling every longer-term learning need.

The scope decision keeps the work focused on what the two starters need first.
It does not establish that other training material is current or approved.

### Testing instructions rather than assuming familiarity

Alex will walk through the material as an experienced Support specialist and
identify steps that depend on unstated knowledge. Screenshots and instructions
can appear straightforward to somebody familiar with the tools while leaving
a new starter uncertain about where to begin or what the step is intended to
accomplish.

The walkthrough is a way to find those gaps, not evidence that new starters
have already completed the guide successfully. Alex's feedback will inform
Riley's revision. Sam's operational check remains separate: instructions can
be easy to follow but still describe an outdated route or an unsupported
practice.

### Separating written guidance from buddy conversations

Participants discussed the difference between a repeatable instruction and
context that is better explored with a buddy. A guide should not assume a
conversation has happened when it has not, but it also should not attempt to
replace every conversation with more text. Riley will distinguish these two
types of material in the refresh.

Buddy prompts should make the need for context visible alongside the relevant
activity. They are not a transfer of policy ownership to the buddy or a reason
to leave basic instructions unclear. The precise wording still needs work and
will be informed by Alex's review of the first-week steps.

### Verifying routing before presenting it as instruction
